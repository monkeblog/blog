Much has been said about how bad social media is for you. I think a specific dynamic I want to highlight is that being constantly exposed to things happening all around the world and to every little neurotic discussion taking place in society trains everyone to either become obsessive neurotic religious white knights or settle into some kind of banal psychopathy. I think the latter is much more common and what normies mostly fall into.

How does this dynamic occur? Consider that in the past, you would mostly be exposed to your local village and its problems. In this scenario, it was feasible to build up a self-image as an upstanding virtuous man, because you could collect evidence of your virtue. You helped your neighbor out when his mother was sick, you donated to church, you bought a meal for a transient, you provided a bed for your friend down on his luck. You were self-sufficient, you grew your own food, you helped others when they needed help, others helped you when you needed it. Maybe you would hear news of far off lands and tragedies but you realistically could not do anything about them and your inaction does not impugn on your character. After all, wouldn't it be even less virtuous to abandon your family and community to maybe marginally help some other cause you only heard about, when your family was right there and needed you then?

***

However, in our more connected world, not only are we constantly exposed to tragedies and evils beyond comprehension, we are given the opportunity to marginally do something about it. You can donate the cost of a cup of coffee to save babies from dying. You can make a social media post raising awareness for your pet cause. You can boycott the corporations that have less-than-scrupulous practices like POISONING BABY FORMULA. However there's just too much and we are all unfortunately exposed as creatures who prioritize our own comfort above all, we demonstrate to ourselves that we would not even (literally!) lift a finger to save a dying child. Thus our self-image of a virtuous person cannot ever be built up, cannot be practiced, and when the time comes that in our (real, offline) personal lives that we require some real virtuous muscle, we find ourselves weak and not up to the task.

---

Virtuousness and morality do not appear out of the ether, they are taught and practiced and cultivated, and expecting peoples who consciously would not spare a couple bucks to save a dying child, to suddenly become great virtuous paragons, is the equivalent of asking a child to deadlift 5 plates.
