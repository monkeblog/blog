I once heard this from a friend who was starting to get into a relationship. They'd been talking for a little while and everything was going great; they really seem like they could get married one day. But she was nervous about a lot of things. "I'm so scared. What if this works out?"

I recently started interviewing for a job that I was really interested in. Like really interesting. It was engaging, it seemed to fit my talents, everything. But as I started thinking about getting this job, I started to think about how I might have to move away, how I'd spend the next years of my 20s for who knows how long instead of the countless other places I could have ended up (I had a pet idea to maybe spend some years in Asia); I, too, started to get scared. What if this worked out?

It sounds kind of funny. Why would you be scared of something working out for you? A lot, as it turns out. The future is always going to be scary even if it's what you want. With any decision you make, you can feel the doors closing behind you as you step into the future, and I think that's always going to be scary. The sort of action that you can't take back. 

You're choosing to going on a path that you don't quite see the end of. That you can see the pitfalls and traps and obstacles ahead but you decide to walk on it anyway. For a relationship or marriage, maybe this is the person you'll make it work with, but maybe they're not perfect in certain ways but that's okay. 

Here's the thing though: it's also beautiful in some way, you know? Making that commitment. It's far worse to sit back and do nothing, and to wait for all the doors to close until there's only one left.

Come to think of it, pretty much every decision we make is crucially consequential. You might be choosing a path, but you're already walking on one no matter what. I think I'm typically scared of things I can't take back, like getting a tattoo. But we're kind of making irreversible decisions all the time anyway, so what's the big deal? Every time I wake up late, every sigh I breathe, every time I forget to put on sunscreen, it all matters. In a way, not making a decision is its own decision, with its own reasons and consequences. Nothing is just another one of the things that you can choose to do. 

Not trying to be scary, though. It's true that everything matters, but you'll burn yourself out if you make yourself super solemn and sour-faced about it. Humour, joy, and happiness are important for a balanced life that also includes serious efforts. Some sort of "ayy lmao" energy that you gotta have, where you follow your heart because it knows what's best in the moment.
